# Coaching Institute Outreach Templates

Copy-paste ready. Fill in `[BRACKETED]` fields for each recipient. Keep each message **short** — Tier-2/3 institute owners are busy running classes, not reading pitch decks.

**Sending discipline:**
- Send 10 emails max per day from a real Gmail (not a mass-sender). Anything more looks like spam.
- Every email must have a **specific** reason it's addressed to them (city, exam focus, batch size). Generic = deleted.
- Follow up **once**, 4 days later. No more than that — you'll burn the address.
- Best time to send: **Tuesday–Thursday, 10 AM IST**. Not Monday (crazy), not Friday (checked out).

## 1. Cold email — first touch

**Subject lines to test (pick one, A/B):**
- `Quick question about [INSTITUTE NAME]'s mock tests`
- `AI mock tests for [CITY]'s JEE aspirants — 15 min?`
- `[FOUNDER FIRST NAME], a question about your test series`

**Body (under 130 words):**

```
Hi [FOUNDER FIRST NAME],

I saw [INSTITUTE NAME] on [SOURCE — Justdial/Google/friend's recommendation]. Looks like you're prepping about [ROUGH BATCH SIZE] students for JEE this year.

Quick question — how much of your faculty's weekend goes into building chapter-wise mock tests?

I built BlueBottleCap — an AI that generates mock tests in your institute's branding in about 40 seconds per test. Full weak-topic maps for every student, exportable PDFs with your logo, and per-seat pricing (₹49–99/seat/month, no setup fee).

We're piloting with 3–4 Tier-2 institutes this quarter. If you're open to a 15-minute demo over WhatsApp video this week, I'll show you the AI generating a Physics mock live — no slides, no pitch.

Worst case, you get 15 minutes of new ideas for your test series.

Reply "yes" and I'll send times.

— Divyanshu
BlueBottleCap · https://bluebottlecap.com/for-institutes
```

**Why each line is there:**
- Line 1: proves it's not a mass email (you know their name and where you found them).
- Line 2: names a real pain (weekend faculty time on mocks).
- Line 3: one-sentence pitch with a specific number (40 seconds) that sticks.
- Line 4: specific price they can compute against seats. Kills "how much does it cost" objection.
- Line 5: scarcity — "3–4 institutes this quarter" is honest and creates FOMO without lying.
- Line 6: "no slides, no pitch" removes the biggest objection (I don't want to sit through a pitch).
- Line 7: "worst case" reframes the risk — even if they don't buy, they get value.
- Line 8: dead-simple reply mechanism.

## 2. Follow-up (4 days later, if no reply)

**Subject:** `Re: [same subject as before]`

```
Hi [FIRST NAME],

Bumping this in case my earlier email got buried.

If it's easier — here's the 60-second demo video: [YOUR YOUTUBE UNLISTED LINK]

You can watch it during a chai break. If it's interesting, reply "call me" and I'll send WhatsApp times.

If not, no worries — I won't email again.

— Divyanshu
```

**Why:**
- Reply-in-thread keeps context, higher open rate than a new email.
- 60-second video removes the friction of scheduling a call to "see if it's interesting."
- "I won't email again" is a commitment that makes them more likely to reply just to say no — and you'd rather have that than uncertainty.

## 3. LinkedIn / WhatsApp version (under 80 words)

Use when you found the person on LinkedIn or a mutual connection shared their WhatsApp.

```
Hi [FIRST NAME] — Divyanshu here, building BlueBottleCap (AI mock tests for coaching centers).

Saw you run [INSTITUTE NAME] in [CITY]. Curious if AI-generated mocks with your branding would be useful for your test series?

Per-seat pricing (₹49–99/seat/mo). Happy to show a 15-min demo whenever — no pitch, just the product.

Worth a look?
```

**Why shorter:**
- LinkedIn/WhatsApp are mobile-scanning contexts. Anything over 4 lines gets skimmed and closed.
- Ends with a low-commitment "worth a look?" — easier to reply "yes" than "yes let's schedule a call."

## 4. Warm reply — "yes, tell me more"

The moment they show interest, DON'T pitch. Send this:

```
Great — three options for the demo, all 15 min on WhatsApp video:

🗓️ [DAY] at [TIME]
🗓️ [DAY] at [TIME]
🗓️ [DAY] at [TIME]

Which works? I'll send the WhatsApp video link 5 min before.

Between now and then, one question that helps me prepare: **what's your test series pain right now — building them, grading them, or students not taking them seriously?**

— Divyanshu
```

**Why:**
- 3 specific times beats "when works?" — reduces decision fatigue.
- The "one question" gets them thinking about the problem before the call, which primes them to actually buy.
- No slides mentioned. No document attached. Feels like a friend, not a vendor.

## 5. After the demo — same day follow-up

```
[FIRST NAME], thanks for the 15 minutes.

Quick recap:
1. Live AI mock generation for [EXAM they care about] — you saw it hit [SPECIFIC MOMENT they reacted to].
2. Per-seat pricing for [SEATS they mentioned] would be **₹[CALCULATED PRICE]/month, total ₹[TOTAL]/month.**
3. Setup takes about a week from a signed pilot agreement.

If you want to pilot with 50 students first (₹99/seat = ₹4,950/mo, cancellable with 30-day notice), reply "let's pilot" and I'll send a one-page agreement.

If it's not a fit right now, I'd love to know why — helps me build a better product.

— Divyanshu
```

**Why:**
- Names the specific moment they reacted — proves you were paying attention, not reading a script.
- Pre-calculated total ₹/month removes the mental math friction.
- Explicit small pilot offer with cancellation clause de-risks the yes.
- The "not a fit — tell me why" line gets you learnings even from losses.

## Where to find leads

1. **Justdial** — filter by "Coaching Classes" + your city. Owner names + numbers visible on many listings.
2. **Sulekha** — same, better for smaller cities.
3. **Google Maps** — search `[city] JEE coaching`, click each pin, note the name + phone.
4. **LinkedIn** — search "founder coaching" OR "director academy" + city. Free tier lets you send ~20 connect requests/week.
5. **College WhatsApp groups** — students share which coaching centers they came from. Warm intro.
6. **Local newspaper ads** — the coaching centers running ads have money to spend. Note names.
